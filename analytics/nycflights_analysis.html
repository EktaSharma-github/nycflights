What makes a flight out of New York late?¶

Every query below runs in PostgreSQL against the nycflights schema; Python only draws the charts and fits one small regression on the rows SQL returns. Data: all 336,776 departures from JFK, LGA and EWR in 2013 (US DOT), with hourly weather, aircraft and airport tables (R package nycflights13).

import os
import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import psycopg
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

DSN = "host=localhost user=postgres password={} dbname=nycflights".format(os.environ.get("PGPASSWORD", "postgres"))
def q(sql):
    with psycopg.connect(DSN) as conn:
        conn.execute("SET search_path = nycflights")
        cur = conn.execute(sql)
        return pd.DataFrame(cur.fetchall(), columns=[d.name for d in cur.description])

wx = q('''SELECT CASE WHEN w.visib_mi < 1 THEN 'Fog (<1 mi)' WHEN w.visib_mi < 5 THEN 'Haze (1-5 mi)' ELSE 'Clear (5+ mi)' END AS visibility,
                 CASE WHEN w.precip_in > 0 THEN 'Rain/snow' ELSE 'Dry' END AS precip,
                 COUNT(*) AS flights, AVG(f.dep_delay)::float AS avg_dep_delay
          FROM flight f JOIN weather w ON (w.origin, w.time_hour) = (f.origin, f.time_hour)
          GROUP BY 1, 2''')
order = ["Clear (5+ mi)", "Haze (1-5 mi)", "Fog (<1 mi)"]
p = wx.pivot(index="visibility", columns="precip", values="avg_dep_delay").loc[order]
x = np.arange(len(order)); w = 0.36
fig, ax = plt.subplots()

5. Putting the suspects side by side¶

One ordinary least-squares regression of departure delay on the factors above. Each row is a flight with a previous leg and a weather reading. Coefficients are in minutes; for the 0/1 factors they read as extra minutes of delay when the factor is present.

In [6]:
df = q('''WITH legs AS (
            SELECT f.*, LAG(f.arr_delay) OVER (PARTITION BY f.tailnum, f.flight_date ORDER BY f.sched_dep_time) AS prev_arr_delay
            FROM flight f WHERE f.tailnum IS NOT NULL AND f.status <> 'Cancelled')
          SELECT l.dep_delay, l.sched_dep_time/100 AS hour, l.prev_arr_delay,
                 (w.precip_in > 0)::int AS rain, (w.visib_mi < 1)::int AS fog,
                 (EXTRACT(MONTH FROM l.flight_date) IN (6,7,12))::int AS peak_month,
                 (l.carrier IN ('EV','FL','MQ','9E'))::int AS regional_low_cost
          FROM legs l JOIN weather w ON (w.origin, w.time_hour) = (l.origin, l.time_hour)
          WHERE l.prev_arr_delay IS NOT NULL''').astype(float)
X = df.drop(columns="dep_delay")
X["prev_late_min"] = X.pop("prev_arr_delay").clip(lower=0)
A = np.column_stack([np.ones(len(X)), X.values])
beta, *_ = np.linalg.lstsq(A, df.dep_delay.values, rcond=None)
resid = df.dep_delay.values - A @ beta
r2 = 1 - resid.var() / df.dep_delay.var()
se = np.sqrt(np.diag(resid.var() * np.linalg.inv(A.T @ A)))
coef = pd.DataFrame({"coef_min": beta, "std_err": se, "t": beta / se}, index=["intercept"] + list(X.columns)).round(3)
print(f"n = {len(df):,} flights   R^2 = {r2:.3f}")
coef

Readings¶

  1. Time of day is the clearest pattern. A 6 am departure leaves 1.6 minutes late on average; a 7 pm departure leaves 24.8 minutes late. The share of delayed flights triples, from 11% to 33%. Delays pile up through the day because each plane carries its earlier lateness forward. Practical advice: fly early.
  2. Airline choice matters, but less than timing. On-time rates run from 64.7% (AirTran) to 81.2% (Virgin America), a 16.5-point spread. The regional carriers that fly short feeder routes for the big airlines (ExpressJet, Envoy, Endeavor) also cancel the most, about 5% of flights.
  3. Rain or snow triples the average delay on a clear day (11 → 33 minutes). Fog adds roughly as much again even when it is dry (28 minutes). Wet weather also lifts cancellations from 1.9% to 7–16%.
  4. Plane age shows no clear effect. Planes aged 10–14 years average 8.3 minutes of arrival delay and planes over 20 years old average 4.1. Older aircraft are mostly flown by the mainline carriers, which are the punctual ones, so airline mixes in with age here.
  5. Knock-on delays are the strongest single driver. When a plane's previous NYC departure that day arrived more than an hour late, its next departure leaves 84 minutes late on average. If the previous one was on time, it leaves 12.6 minutes late (r = 0.375).
  6. The regression puts them side by side. It covers 78,822 flights that have both a previous leg and a weather reading. Each minute of earlier lateness adds 0.6 minutes (t = 108), rain adds 23 minutes, fog 31, a peak month (June, July or December) 10, each later hour 0.8, and a regional or low-cost carrier 3.5. R² is 0.18: most of the variation in delay comes from things this data does not record, such as air-traffic-control holds and crew problems.
  7. Late departures recover a little in the air: about 2 minutes on short routes and 7 minutes on routes over 1,500 miles.

Limits. This is observational data from one year at one city's airports, so these are associations, not causes. Planes missing from planes.csv (mostly Envoy and American) are left out of the age analysis.
